# 2026-10-01: the stream on the real-time clock

The user's direction: the reasoning is to be "synced to the microsecond
in relation to the semantic chain of reason on the real-time clock",
not paced by the clock speed of the cards. Until now everything was
counted in cycles, whose pace is whatever the GPU and the cards give:
the nudges every 256 tokens, no times in the logs, no time in the
model's context. Now the wall clock is the reference frame (`src/clock.md`).

## What changed

- Every piece of the stream is stamped with the microsecond it exists
  at (`CLOCK_REALTIME`), in `chain.log` (`t_us<TAB>kind<TAB>text`) and on
  the socket (`text KIND t=US TEXT`); every reading of the mind with the
  microsecond its token's decode finished (`mind t=US ...`); every status
  with the microsecond it was taken.
- The chain carries the time where it matters to the mind: the opening
  its date and time (`« [2026-10-01 13:35:56.722141 CDT] ...`), every
  line from outside the microsecond it was heard or handed over, every
  line from the system the microsecond it was written, a rollover base
  its date and time, and after `--time-every` seconds with nothing from
  outside the time and the length of the quiet.
- The intervals the engine acts on are real time on the monotonic clock:
  the quiet before the clock is put in, the spacing of nudges
  (`--nudge-every`, seconds, in place of 256 tokens), and a new one, the
  spacing of failed reads put into the chain (below).
- The terminal's title shows the stream's clock (the time of its newest
  piece) to the microsecond; the mind view shows each reading's time.

## Live (`~/.cache/phi-asm-test/stream/clock-live/`)

The service with the mind and `--time-every 20`, a line said 16 s in,
75 s in all. From the workspace's `stream.log`:

```
« [2026-10-01 13:35:56.722141 CDT] (the room is quiet; nothing has been said. ...)
« [13:36:12.306484] what time is it where you are, and how long have we been talking?
« [13:36:34.126512] (nothing from outside for 20.0 s)
```

`chain.log`: every piece stamped; over its last 200 pieces, 45 ms apart
on average, at most 281 ms. 432 readings of the mind, each with its
microsecond. The status at the end: 37.6 tokens a second, a 29 ms cycle.

## A loop found and closed

The live run showed a feedback loop the clock made visible: the mind
wrote `[read: PATH]` for paths it invented (`/home/den/.config/journal/
threads.json`, `/memory/memory.json`); each failure put a line from the
system into the chain, which prompted another guess, three in 18
seconds. A failed read now goes into the chain at most once per
`--nudge-every` interval; the others are notes outside it, counted in
the status (`reads_quiet`). The same run showed the journal frame's known
leak (a `«` line in the visitor's voice, now with a time) and the mind
imitating a line from the system (`« [..] [from the memory file: ...`);
both are counted as leaks, as before.
