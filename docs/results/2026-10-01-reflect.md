## The gate: every state kept, every row its own lane's

`scripts/phi-stream.sh gate --reflect` (`src/gate.md`) runs 8 episodes,
4 kept and 4 rewound, each from a fresh prompt, as the engine runs them:
1. S (the snapshot) and D (the deliberation) are copied before the
   chosen token.
2. D's first token goes alone.
3. 16 cycles decode the live token and D's lane in one batch, the live
   lane first.
4. The episode is kept, or rewound onto S.

The capture is installed with blocks 27, 29, 31 and the final one.

In every decode of the final run, each captured output row, read as it
is, gives llama's logits for its own lane: identity difference 0.000000
throughout. This proves what the mind's first-row rule rests on.

For the states, four controls were tried; the record keeps all four.

| run | control | live | D | after | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (`gate-reflect.run1.out`) | the same tokens one by one, no copy | max\|d\| 0.32 to 1.12 | 0.25 to 0.81 | | uncalibrated limit 0.5: meaningless (the composition gate had measured 0.65 for chunking alone) |
| 2 (`gate-reflect.run2.out`) | as 1, KL against a floor of one-by-one vs pairs | KL up to 0.0068 | | | under the floor, but a one-token mix-up gave KL 0.0083: the statistic could not resolve it |
| 3 (`gate-reflect.run3.out`) | D prefilled separately, same shapes | max\|d\| 0.23 to 1.05 | 0.87 to 1.65 | **0.0000 on the 4 rewinds** | the snapshot exact; live and D moved by the KV layout (D's prefix in other cells: another attention reduction order) |
| 4 (`gate-reflect.out`) | the same decodes in the same cells, D copied as in the run, no snapshot | **0.0000** | **0.0000** | **0.0000** | **PASS**: bit-exact on all 8 episodes |

In the final run, the control reproduced itself to 0.0000: the backend,
cards included, is bit-deterministic for identical decodes in identical
cells. One token different (the chosen token placed instead of the
second best) moved the logits by 8.39 at the least and 17.9 at the most,
839 times the limit.

The path under test is the live sequence sharing its recurrent cell
with the snapshot and copy-on-writing inside a two-lane ubatch
(`llama_memory_recurrent::find_slot`, read, not modified). It changes
nothing, to the bit.

## Live: the first answer protocol failed, visibly

The setup for every live run:
- `phi-stream run --mind --reflect --seed 7 --max-tokens 1200`;
- the journal frame with the user's `~/CLAUDE.md` as the base;
- temperature sampling;
- the horizon at 1 s.

The opening carries the wall clock to the microsecond, so no two runs
generate the same text: the A/B below compares the same process, not
the same text.

The first protocol asked for "One line: keep, or write: TOKEN, and why"
(`live-v1.err`, `live-v2.err`). All 12 checks of each run came back
`unparsed`: the model wrote "TOKEN, because the next token is \"The\"
...", "TOKEN. \"determined\" is the wrong word here. The trajectory is
not determined by the ...". It reasoned about the word but copied the
placeholder instead of answering. Every check ran to its 24-token cap
(1172 to 1404 ms), with 24 live tokens placed meanwhile.

The second protocol ends the question on `Decision:` and reads the
choice from D's distribution: keep's share of keep and write, summed
over each word's one-token forms (`src/reflect.md`).

| run | checks | outcome | ms a check | live tokens meanwhile | keep share | keep and write's share of everything (`fmt`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| v3 (`live-v3.err`) | 12 | 12 kept | 372 to 417 | 1 | mean 0.900 (0.50 to 0.99) | mean 0.332 (0.17 to 0.47) |
| v4, the forms summed (`live-v4.err`, 700 tokens) | 12 | 12 kept | 386 to 441 | 1 | mean 0.863 | mean 0.368 |

How the loop fired, in the journal at temperature:
- **The doubt trigger** fires at the budget's rate, a check every 2 s
  (the refractory period) until the 12 a minute are spent. Sampled
  words with the model's own probability under 0.3, and not among the
  band's top five, are common in prose.
- **The model kept every one**, mostly with confidence above 0.85.
- **The flag trigger never fired**: the smoothed error-word mass stayed
  under 0.03.
- **On `fmt`**: between a third and a half of the model's mass after
  `Decision:` falls on neither word. Summing the case forms raised it
  little. It is recorded per episode as the measure of how far the model
  answers the question at all.

In prose the triggers are loose and the budget is what limits them, as
section 8 of the plan expects of a first setting. The sweep that sets
the balance needs the code measurement and the firing audit below.

## The display's own clock (after BF++'s scene oracle)

The placement jumps: 25 ms a token, 35 ms beside a check, a 344 ms cycle
for a question, nothing while a check holds its token. The engine's
thread is inside `llama_decode` during those cycles, so pacing there
cannot help.

BF++'s scene oracle (`runtime/bfpp_rt_3d_oracle.c` in Lasimeri/bfpp)
decouples a producer's rate from a consumer's:
- a consumer thread;
- a buffer that absorbs the mismatch;
- graceful degradation as the data ages;
- a clamp against runaway.

`src/playout.rs` is that, for text: the display runs about a horizon
behind the placement, at the arrival pace, slower as its buffer drains,
faster as it fills, never more than twice the horizon behind.

The run's stdout was measured through `tools/stamp.c`, as a reader sees
it (gaps between arrivals after the first 5 s):

| binary | display | answer protocol | median | p90 | p99 | max | gaps >= 100 ms | >= 250 ms | >= 500 ms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| v1 | each piece at placed + 1 s | free line (checks 1.2 to 1.4 s) | 30.0 | 51.8 | 283.6 | 419.0 | 30 | 13 | 0 |
| v2 | playout, with a floor of a quarter horizon | free line | 28.0 | 36.5 | 55.2 | 608.0 | 10 | 10 | 5 |
| v3 | playout, no floor | the choice (checks 0.4 s) | 28.8 | 37.4 | 47.4 | 53.3 | **0** | 0 | 0 |
| v4 | as v3 | the choice, forms summed | 33.8 | 43.0 | 53.6 | 56.8 | **0** | 0 | 0 |
| v5, `--horizon 0` (no playout, `live-h0`) | as placed | the choice (checks 359 to 429 ms) | 24.5 | 35.3 | 87.2 | 509.1 | 10 | 10 | 1 |
| v5, every check made to write (`live-write`, 600 tokens) | playout | the choice, `--reflect-keep-at 1.01` | 27.8 | 37.5 | 52.9 | 56.7 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The fixed hold shows every check that outlasts it as a stall. The
playout's first version (v2) evened the flow (p99 down from 284 to
55 ms) but made the stalls of the long checks worse. Its floor held the
text that arrived late, after a check, another quarter horizon on top of
the stall, which a starving display only lengthens. The floor serves
nothing now that taking back is the engine's (a check holds its token),
so it went.

With the floor gone and checks of 0.4 s, the largest gap a reader sees
in 32 s of the stream with 12 checks is 53 ms. The attribution: the same
checks shown as placed (`--horizon 0`) leave 10 gaps of a quarter second
or more, the largest 509 ms. The shorter checks alone do not make the
stream seamless; the playout does. Tokens are placed at 25
to 35 ms, so that is seamless.

## The engine's rewind, forced

The gate proves the lanes; the engine's own rewind is more than that:
- the hold cut at the token;
- the placement state restored;
- the sequences swapped;
- the sampler rebuilt;
- the word's other tokens forced.

The live checks all kept, so that path had not run. `--reflect-keep-at
1.01` makes every check write (`live-write`, v5, 600 tokens). Of 6
checks:
- **5 came back `same`**: made to write, the model wrote the word it had
  chosen (`write: "The"`, `write: mechanism.`).
- **1 changed**: " has" became " nothing" at position 1703, taking back
  positions 1703 to 1708.
- **1 was `abandoned`**: the run ended during it.

`chain.log` holds no piece placed between that token and the rewind (0
in the check's window), and the first piece after the window is
" nothing". The text taken back never went out, and the stream ran on to
its 600 tokens. Checks that write cost 543 to 584 ms (4 or 5 live tokens
placed meanwhile).

**Where `fmt`'s missing mass goes.** The deliberation's three likeliest
tokens at `Decision:` (`top1`..`top3` in each episode) show it:
- an opening quote `"` (0.16 to 0.29): the model starting to quote a
  word;
- the chosen word itself (" mechanism", " The"): answering with the word
  rather than the verb;
- ` keep` (0.15 to 0.53), and ` Keep`.

So the choice is made over a third to a half of the distribution. Its
other mass leans toward restating the word, which is a keep.

## BF++, what else carries over

- **The check is a result block in BF++'s sense** (`R{...}K{...}`). Its
  error register is saved before the block, and a catch restores it:
  - the engine's `Saved` (the placement state at the token) is that
    register;
  - the snapshot S is the tape;
  - a write is the catch that restores both and places another word.
- **"Produces identical results to the CPU path"**, BF++'s rule for its
  GPU-accelerated lexer, is the standard `gate --reflect` now meets:
  bit-exact, not "within noise".
- **To carry over next**:
  - BF++'s double-buffered, diff-based terminal rendering (`__tui_begin`
    and `__tui_end`: only the cells that changed are emitted) for the
    terminal, which redraws its whole screen today;
  - its tape checkpoints (`__tape_save` and `__tape_load`) as a
    checkpoint of the live sequence's state on disk, so a restarted
    stream continues instead of beginning again.
